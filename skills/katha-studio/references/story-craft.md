# Story craft and shot rules

## The structure that holds viewers

1. Hook (first 15 to 20 s): the most emotional moment or question of the story, before the title. Example: a poor man at a golden gate who is not sure his friend will know him.
2. The world: who the people are and what they want. Keep it short.
3. The story as dialogue: the characters speak; the narrator only links scenes and covers time jumps. In the best channels of this kind, most of the screen time is characters talking.
4. The turn: the moment of grace, test or miracle.
5. The moral in 1 or 2 lines, then a closing line of devotion that fits the tradition of the story.

## Length and count

| Film length | Scenes | Talk scenes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 min | 8 to 10 | 4 to 6 |
| 5 min | 35 to 45 | 20 to 28 |
| 20 min | 130 to 160 | 80 to 100 |

One scene is one line by one speaker. A line of 6 to 12 words fits a talk shot.

## Writing the lines

- Write as people speak, not as a book. Short sentences.
- Use the names often, so a viewer who joins late knows who is who.
- Keep the source honest. Name the text the story comes from (Bhagavata Purana, Ramayana, a bhakta katha) in the YouTube description.
- Use punctuation: captions split on । . ! ? and commas.

## Picture prompts

- Every talk scene: "Front-facing medium close-up portrait of {id} looking at the camera, <emotion>, <place> softly blurred. Clear frontal face, mouth closed, head and shoulders in frame."
- Every still scene: name the shot (wide, medium, close-up), the place, the light, what each character does, and the emotion.
- Change the shot size from scene to scene: wide, medium, close-up. The same framing many times looks like a slideshow.
- Put at most 2 or 3 characters in one picture. More characters means more faces that drift.
- Name a place the same way each time ("the golden palace hall of Dwarka with white marble pillars").

## Faults to prevent in the text

| Fault | Prevent it with |
|---|---|
| An adult god drawn as a child | "an adult man about 30 years old (not a child), adult proportions" |
| A poor character with a crown or jewels | "no crown, no jewellery" in that character's look |
| Text, letters or fake signs in the picture | The style already says "no text"; do not describe signs or books with writing |
| A talk shot with a profile face | "front-facing, looking at the camera, clear frontal face" |
| Extra people | "only these 2 people in the frame" |
